The guide uses two recurring devices. A grey italic **rationale** note explains the thinking
behind a decision already established. A gold-edged **enhancement** note marks a
recommendation *added* in Edition 02 — a proposal for review, not approved rule. Enhancements
are labelled as such below.

**Logo system status:** deliberately removed pending a finished mark. Vehicle livery specs and

## 01 · Brand Overview & Strategy

**Mission** — To make every journey between two points feel considered: private, punctual and
genuinely welcoming, whether that's a 6 a.m. run to JFK or a wedding motorcade through Brooklyn.

**Vision** — To be the first name that comes to mind for chauffeured travel in New York City —
the trusted call for anyone who needs a car to simply be there, be right, and be discreet about it.

**Core values**

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Punctuality as respect | On time is not a metric — it's how the brand shows it values a client's time as much as its own |
| Discretion by default | Conversations, routes and client details stay in the car. Nothing is assumed shareable |
| Craft in the details | Clean interior, correct temperature, a driver who already knows the terminal |
| Warm professionalism | Formal enough for a boardroom, human enough for a wedding morning |
| One standard, every borough | All five boroughs run to the same bar — no borough gets the diluted version |

**Positioning** — *RSSkyler Limo is New York City's accessible-luxury chauffeur service — the
confidence of a five-star hotel car, without the velvet-rope distance.*

"Accessible luxury" is the central strategic bet: premium enough for a high-net-worth client,
approachable enough that a first-time corporate booker never feels out of place calling. Every
later decision — the warmth in the voice, the restraint in the gold accent, the plain-language
app copy — is downstream of this one bet.

*Enhancement, unresolved:* an internal brand-kit draft named three pillars — reliability,
exclusivity, professionalism. Reliability and professionalism map onto the values above.
**Exclusivity is genuinely new and sits in real tension with accessible luxury.** The guide's
position: if the brand wants to lean exclusive that is a legitimate direction, but it must be
chosen deliberately, not layered on top of a positioning statement that promises the opposite.
Until that decision is made, do not write exclusivity into copy.

*Enhancement:* a short-form internal version of this chapter for chauffeurs and dispatch —
*Punctual by design, Discreet by nature, Local at heart.*

**Who we serve**

| Segment | Needs | Earns trust |
|---|---|---|
| Business travellers | Reliable airport & point-to-point transfers, often booked by an assistant | Live tracking, flight-aware pickup, zero-friction billing |
| Diplomatic & executive | Discreet chauffeur-led travel for consulates, missions, corporate leadership | Familiar drivers, verified vehicles, quiet consistency over time |
| Corporate accounts | A dependable fleet for staff and visiting executives | Monthly invoicing, SLAs, a single point of contact |
| Wedding & event hosts | Coordinated multi-vehicle logistics for a day that cannot slip | A dedicated planner, rehearsed timing, calm on-site presence |

**Differentiators** — accessible luxury · tech + personal service · local expertise across five
boroughs · authentic imagery · a dedicated wedding & events division · one system, every touchpoint.

## 02 · Color Palette

Five colours, one disciplined hierarchy. Midnight blue and gold, built for restraint rather
than excess.

| Name | Hex | RGB | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Midnight Blue | `#0B2142` | 11, 33, 66 | Primary · ~60% of any layout |
| Accent Gold ("Golden Grass") | `#D4A017` | 212, 160, 23 · HSL 43°, 80%, 46% | Accent · ~10%, highlights & CTAs only |
| Charcoal | `#2C2C2F` | 44, 44, 47 | Secondary text & UI |
| Pure White | `#FFFFFF` | — | Background · ~30%, readability |
| Light Grey | `#F5F5F5` | — | Secondary backgrounds, panels |

*Rationale:* midnight blue signals trust and professionalism without the coldness of black.
Gold adds luxury in small deliberate doses rather than ostentation. The 60/30/10 ratio keeps
every layout calm — gold stays a highlight, never a field colour.

**Five colours is the entire palette, by design.** Where a layout seems to need a sixth — a
hairline, a disabled state, a hover tint — derive it as a tint or low-opacity tone of one of
the five. Consistency compounds; a growing colour list quietly erodes it.

**Contrast**, measured against WCAG 2.1 (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text and UI):

| Combination | Ratio | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Midnight on white | 16.0:1 | AAA |
| White on midnight | 16.0:1 | AAA |
| Charcoal on white | 13.9:1 | AAA |
| Midnight on light grey | 14.7:1 | AAA |
| Gold on midnight | 6.8:1 | AA, close to AAA — safe for body and UI text |
| Gold on white | 2.4:1 | **Fails AA at every size — graphic use only** |

*Enhancement, adopted throughout:* the original spec called for "gold background, white text"
CTAs — white on gold measures ~1.9:1. Rebuild gold buttons as **gold fill with midnight text**
(9.9:1, AAA), or keep white text and move it onto a midnight or charcoal fill using gold only
for the border, icon or underline. Gold should appear as text only when sitting on midnight or
charcoal; everywhere else let it be a line, an icon, a small fill, or a large decorative numeral.

**Surfaces** — *Primary:* midnight ground, white text, gold reserved for icons and small
accents. *Light:* white or grey ground, charcoal or midnight text, gold as border or icon only.

## 03 · Typography

A serif for heritage, a sans for the road ahead. Two typefaces, each doing one job well.

**Fraunces** — warm display serif with real optical character; evokes heritage and
craftsmanship without the stiffness of a strictly formal serif. H1–H3, pull quotes, and
rationale notes (set italic).

**Public Sans** — clean humanist sans built for legibility at small sizes. Body copy, UI
labels, tables, invoices. Carries the brand's modern, digital-native side.

*Rationale:* serif headlines evoke premium heritage — the same register as luxury hospitality
and automotive brands. Sans-serif body keeps the app, invoices and booking flow easy to scan on
a phone at a curb. Together they balance tradition with everyday usability.

| Role | Typeface / weight | Size |
|---|---|---|
| H1 — chapter / page title | Fraunces Semibold | 34–40px |
| H2 — section title | Fraunces Semibold | 22–26px |
| H3 — subsection | Public Sans Semibold | 16–18px |
| Body | Public Sans Regular | 15–16px / 1.65–1.7 line-height |
| UI label / caption | Public Sans Medium | 12–13px |
| Rationale / pull-quote | Fraunces Italic | 15–17px |

**Numerals** — use tabular (fixed-width) figures for anything set in a column or compared at a
glance: pricing, invoices, flight times, itineraries.

*Enhancement:* treat **16px as the practical floor for anything read outdoors** — greeting
cards, door signage, a driver's in-app name display. Fraunces' fine serif detail can soften
under headlights or phone-screen glare at night.

## 04 · Brand Voice & Messaging

Confident enough for a boardroom, warm enough for a wedding morning.

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Confident | States things plainly; never hedges on what the client can expect |
| Professional | Correct, considered, never sloppy |
| Welcoming | Speaks to a person, not an account number |
| Discreet | Says only what is needed; never performs privacy, just practises it |
| Efficient | Short sentences. No filler. Respects the reader's time in writing, too |

*Rationale:* the voice balances the formality high-net-worth clients expect with the
approachability modern consumers demand — avoiding both the coldness of an overly formal
luxury brand and the over-familiarity of a consumer app.

**Messaging pillars** — *Effortless from the first tap* (booking, tracking and billing should
feel simpler than the trip itself) · *Discreet excellence* (the best compliment is a client who
never had to think about the logistics at all) · *Local mastery, five boroughs deep* (Manhattan
to JFK to a Brooklyn brownstone, genuinely known — not a midtown-only playbook stretched thin).

**Taglines**

| Tagline | Best used for |
|---|---|
| *Arrive in Style* | Primary — homepage, app, general marketing |
| *Your City, Chauffeured* | Corporate & commuter-facing materials |
| *Precision. Privacy. Presence.* | Diplomatic, executive & high-discretion clients |
| *Every Detail, Minded* | Wedding & events division |

**Voice in practice**

Say: "Your driver is five minutes out." · "We've got your flight tracked — no need to call." ·
"Confirmed for 6:00 a.m. See you then."

Avoid: "Your transportation solution is being dispatched." · "OMG your ride is almost here!! 🎉" ·
unnecessary exclamation points, corporate jargon, forced slang.

*Enhancement — sample copy, drafted in-voice:*

| Moment | Copy |
|---|---|
| Booking confirmation | "You're confirmed for Thursday, 6:00 a.m. — JFK, Terminal 4. Your driver will be waiting at arrivals." |
| Flight delay detected | "We saw your flight moved to 9:40 p.m. Your pickup has been updated automatically — nothing you need to do." |
| Wedding morning | "Good morning — today's motorcade is set and confirmed. Your coordinator is on-site from 9:00 a.m." |
| Social caption | "Same driver, same standard, every time you ride." |

## 05 · Imagery & Photography

The camera should find the brand in real life, not stage it. Authentic over aspirational:
RSSkyler's own chauffeurs, own fleet, recognisable New York City locations.

*Rationale:* a client who has already ridden with RSSkyler should recognise the car, the
street, sometimes even the driver, in the brand's own marketing. That recognition builds more
trust than any styled studio shot.

| Aspect | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Colour grade | Cool, slightly desaturated base with midnight blue in the shadows — never warm or orange-heavy |
| Light | Golden hour and blue hour for exterior vehicle shots — the gold-on-midnight relationship, found again in the sky |
| Composition | Give the vehicle and the city room to breathe; avoid tight, cluttered crops |
| People | Candid over posed — a chauffeur opening a door mid-motion reads more true than a static portrait |

Use: own fleet and chauffeurs on real routes · recognisable New York landmarks and streetscapes
(midtown, the bridges, the outer boroughs) · natural light, golden or blue hour.

Avoid: stock photography of unrelated luxury cars or generic models · heavy filters, vignettes
or lens flares in post · studio-white backgrounds that could belong to any brand.

*Enhancement:* represent the whole city, not just Manhattan. A livery shot outside a Brooklyn
brownstone or at LaGuardia carries the same brand weight as one on Park Avenue. Cast and crew
should reflect who actually rides and who actually drives in New York.

## 06 · Fleet & Service Positioning

| Class | Best for |
|---|---|
| Luxury Sedan | Individual and two-passenger travel — airport transfers, point-to-point, executive pickups |
| Luxury SUV | Small groups and extra luggage, without stepping up to a full premium class |
| Premium SUV | The top of the fleet — VIP, diplomatic and flagship corporate bookings |
| Sprinter Van | Group transport, wedding parties, and event logistics |

| Service | Detail |
|---|---|
| Airport Transfers | Flight-tracked pickup and drop-off across JFK, LaGuardia and Newark |
| Hourly Charters | A car and driver on standby for meetings, appointments, or a flexible day |
| Corporate Accounts | Monthly billing, SLAs and a dedicated contact for company travel programs |
| Events | Coordinated multi-vehicle logistics for conferences, galas and private functions |
| Weddings | A dedicated division — see Chapter 10 |

*Enhancement:* "RSSkyler" already carries the word *Sky* — a naming convention playing on it
would make the fleet easier to sell and remember: **Sky Sedan, Sky SUV, Sky SUV Premium, Sky
Sprinter**. It also opens a three-tier loyalty program — *Sky Silver, Sky Gold, Sky Platinum* —
reusing the palette's own gold accent as a literal status marker.

## 07 · Digital Experience

Modern booking technology, wrapped around a personal service.

| Principle | Detail |
|---|---|
| Clarity first | Fare, vehicle class and pickup time are visible before the client has to ask |
| Few taps to book | The booking flow should feel shorter than describing the trip out loud |
| Calm under pressure | Delays, driver changes and reroutes are communicated automatically, plainly, ahead of the client noticing something is wrong |
| Consistent chrome | Midnight header, white content, gold reserved for the primary action only — on every screen |

| Page | Job to be done |
|---|---|
| Home / booking | Get a fare and a confirmed pickup in under a minute |
| Fleet | Show each vehicle class plainly enough to choose without calling support |
| Corporate | Explain accounts, billing and SLAs to a travel manager evaluating vendors |
| Weddings & Events | Feel like its own considered experience |
| Trip tracking | Answer "where is my car" before the client opens the app to ask |

*Enhancement — product moves that would reinforce the brand promise rather than describe it:*
upfront fixed pricing (full fare before booking, not an estimate — the single biggest trust gap
in ground transportation) · live map tracking shareable with a third party who doesn't have the
app · corporate platform integration (Concur, TripActions) so approval and expensing happen
without a separate login · saved profiles (preferred vehicle class, temperature, route notes) ·
SMS handoff at the curb for guests without the app.

## 08 · Physical Applications

**Uniforms** — charcoal or midnight blue, tailored and understated. Never a branded T-shirt
treatment. Gold appears only as thin piping or a pin, never as fabric colour.

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Business cards | Midnight blue background, gold accent |
| Letterhead | White background, midnight blue header |
| Brochures | White background, midnight blue headers |
| Invoices | Clean white, branded header, tabular figures throughout |
| Packaging | Midnight blue boxes, gold foil accent |

*Enhancement:* the **arrivals greeting** is the one physical touchpoint clients actually
photograph. A branded tablet or printed card — midnight background, guest name set in Fraunces —
replaces the generic paper sign most car services hand a driver.

*Pending:* vehicle livery (logo placement, minimum size) is held back with the logo system, and
should be the first section restored once a mark is finalised — it is the most visible physical
application the brand has.

## 09 · Visual Applications & Templates

| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Primary buttons | Midnight blue background, white text |
| Secondary buttons | White background, midnight blue border and text |
| CTAs / highlights | Gold background, **midnight text** — corrected from white per Chapter 2 |
| Links | Midnight blue, underlined on hover |
| Success states | Green (universal convention) |
| Error states | Red (universal convention) |

**Social templates** — Instagram Post 1080×1080: full-bleed vehicle photo, midnight gradient
overlay at base, white headline, gold accent line, gold "Book Now" button. Instagram Story
1080×1920: full-screen photo, minimal text in top or bottom third, "Book Now" sticker. LinkedIn
Banner 1584×396: midnight gradient or cityscape, "Premium Chauffeur Service" headline, "New York
City · 24/7" subhead.

**Email** — *Booking confirmation:* midnight header · white body with clear booking details ·
gold "View Booking" button · footer with contact and social links · tone professional,
reassuring, helpful. *Promotional:* hero image · clear headline and value proposition ·
prominent "Book Now" button · footer with contact, social and preferences link.

**Presentation** — 16:9 · white or light-grey background · midnight serif headers · sans body ·
gold accent lines.

*Rationale:* every application traces back to the same five colours and two typefaces. That
repetition is the point — a client should recognise RSSkyler from a cropped Instagram Story, a
business card glimpsed on a desk, or an email footer, on palette and type alone, even before a
finished mark is in place.

*Pending:* app icon and favicon specs. Once a mark is locked, plan a 1024×1024 master plus a
deliberately simplified favicon cut — fine detail rarely survives down to 32px.

## 10 · Wedding & Events Division

The most photographed day the brand will ever take part in.

| Tier | Includes |
|---|---|
| Classic | One Luxury Sedan or SUV for the couple, half-day booking |
| Signature | Couple's vehicle plus a Sprinter Van for the wedding party, full-day booking, dedicated on-site coordinator |
| Bespoke | Full motorcade across multiple vehicle classes, multi-day availability for pre-wedding events, a named coordinator from first call to final drop-off |

The division borrows RSSkyler's full visual system — same palette, same typefaces — but the
voice warms further: more present tense, more sensory detail, less operational language than the
core brand uses elsewhere. Photography leans further into golden hour by design, not exception.

*Enhancement:* give the division a light sub-brand name — *RSSkyler Weddings*, set in smaller
Fraunces italic beneath the primary name — so it reads as a specialised extension rather than a
generic add-on. Pair with a referral program for planners and venues.

## 11 · Brand Governance

**Approval** — any new client-facing material (print, digital, vehicle, uniform) is checked
against this guide before production. Anything that departs from it — a new colour, an unlisted
visual treatment, an off-voice headline — routes to the marketing owner for sign-off before it ships.

**Assets** — all approved brand assets live in a single centralised DAM, not scattered across
laptops and email threads. Agencies and print vendors get pointed at that one source.

**Audits** — a standing audit on a regular cadence: livery, uniforms, stationery, app screens
and social templates checked side-by-side against this guide, so drift is caught at the level of
a single vehicle rather than an entire fleet.

*Enhancement:* version-control every guideline export with an edition number and date, exactly
as this document does · include a one-page version of Chapters 1 and 5 in every new chauffeur
and dispatcher's first-day materials.

## 12 · Strategic Recommendations

Business-level proposals, explicitly **not yet standing guidance**.

1. **A loyalty program built from the palette already in hand** — *Sky Silver, Sky Gold, Sky
   Platinum*. Visible status that costs nothing new to design, since it reuses the gold accent.
   Platinum could carry the saved-profile and priority-dispatch features from Chapter 7.
2. **A playbook for New York's recurring demand spikes** — UN General Assembly week, Fashion
   Week, awards season, Thanksgiving through New Year's Eve, Javits Center conventions. A
   standing calendar with pre-booked fleet availability and premium-period pricing turns each
   from an operational scramble into a planned, more profitable event.
3. **Make trust and safety a stated differentiator, not an assumption** — a short, plainly
   written page covering chauffeur background-check policy, vehicle inspection cadence,
   insurance coverage and confidentiality practices. Matters directly to the diplomatic and
   executive segment; costs little to publish once written down.
4. **Corporate & concierge partnerships** — hotel concierge desks, wedding planners, event
   agencies and corporate travel managers already refer clients before those clients search
   independently. A standing referral or commission structure turns each into a low-cost
   acquisition channel.
5. **A sustainability position, stated honestly** — corporate procurement increasingly weighs
   vendor sustainability. If any portion of the fleet is hybrid or electric, that deserves a
   line in Chapter 6. If it isn't yet, a stated timeline is more credible than an unstated
   aspiration.

## Wordmark & Lockups (companion document, 05.1)

The wordmark spells the business out in full — it carries the name on first contact (search
results, a car door, an invoice, an email footer) where a monogram alone would say nothing to
someone who has not booked before.

- **Primary · light** — `RSSKYLERLIMO` entirely in midnight, on white and light-grey grounds.
- **Primary · dark** — `RSSKYLER` in white, `LIMO` in gold, on midnight and charcoal grounds.

*How it is built:* one word, two weights. "RSSKYLER" in Fraunces Semibold and "LIMO" in
Fraunces Regular, on a single baseline with **4.8% letter-spacing**. The weight shift lets the
eye read two parts of a twelve-letter name without inserting a space the brand does not have;
the added tracking gives capitals the air they need at display size.

Capitals set the register — in caps the name reads as an established house rather than a
start-up, the same register as the luxury hospitality and automotive brands this business sits
beside. A lowercase domain line beneath supplies contrast so the lockup never becomes a wall of
capitals.
